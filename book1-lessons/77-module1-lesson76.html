Introduction · Introducción

Question tags are short questions added to the end of a statement, used to confirm information or invite agreement — like "isn't it?" or "don't you?" They're extremely common in natural spoken English.

Las question tags son preguntas cortas agregadas al final de una afirmación, usadas para confirmar información o invitar a estar de acuerdo — como "isn't it?" o "don't you?" Son extremadamente comunes en el inglés hablado natural.

Did you know? / ¿Sabías qué?

The golden rule of question tags is simple: positive statement gets a negative tag, and negative statement gets a positive tag — they always go in opposite directions.

La regla de oro de las question tags es simple: afirmación positiva lleva coletilla negativa, y afirmación negativa lleva coletilla positiva — siempre van en direcciones opuestas.

Pronunciation · Pronunciación
rising ↗ (real question)
falling ↘ (just confirming)

Rising intonation on a question tag (↗) means you're genuinely unsure and asking. Falling intonation (↘) means you're almost certain and just inviting agreement. / La entonación ascendente en una question tag (↗) significa que realmente no estás seguro y estás preguntando. La entonación descendente (↘) significa que estás casi seguro y solo invitas a estar de acuerdo.

"You are tired, are you?" (same polarity as the statement)
"You are tired, aren't you?" (opposite polarity: positive statement + negative tag)
6
Grammar · Building the tag: auxiliary + pronoun

Copy the auxiliary verb from the statement (or add do/does/did if there's none) and flip its polarity, then add the matching subject pronoun: "Maria is nice, isn't she?" "You like coffee, don't you?"

Copia el verbo auxiliar de la afirmación (o agrega do/does/did si no hay ninguno) y invierte su polaridad, luego agrega el pronombre sujeto correspondiente: "Maria is nice, isn't she?" "You like coffee, don't you?"
