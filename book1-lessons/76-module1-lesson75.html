Introduction · Introducción

A gerund is a verb + "-ing" used as a noun (swimming, reading). An infinitive is "to" + base verb (to swim, to read). Some verbs are followed only by one form, some by both — and a few even change meaning depending on which one you use.

Un gerundio es un verbo + "-ing" usado como sustantivo (swimming, reading). Un infinitivo es "to" + verbo base (to swim, to read). Algunos verbos van seguidos solo de una forma, algunos de ambas — y unos pocos hasta cambian de significado según cuál uses.

Did you know? / ¿Sabías qué?

"Stop smoking" means quitting a habit, but "stop to smoke" means pausing another activity in order to smoke — the exact same verb, two completely different meanings!

"Stop smoking" significa dejar un hábito, pero "stop to smoke" significa pausar otra actividad para fumar — ¡el mismo verbo exacto, dos significados completamente diferentes!

Pronunciation · Pronunciación
remember doing (past memory)
remember to do (future task)

"Remember doing" refers to a memory of the past ("I remember locking the door"), while "remember to do" refers to a future task not yet completed ("Remember to lock the door"). / "Remember doing" se refiere a un recuerdo del pasado ("I remember locking the door"), mientras que "remember to do" se refiere a una tarea futura aún no completada ("Remember to lock the door").

"I enjoy to read" / "I want swimming"
"I enjoy reading" (enjoy takes gerund) / "I want to swim" (want takes infinitive)
6
Grammar · Which verbs take which form

Gerund only: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, keep, practice. Infinitive only: want, decide, promise, agree, plan, hope, refuse. Both, same meaning: like, love, hate, start, begin, continue. Both, different meaning: stop, remember, forget, try.

Solo gerundio: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, keep, practice. Solo infinitivo: want, decide, promise, agree, plan, hope, refuse. Ambos, mismo significado: like, love, hate, start, begin, continue. Ambos, significado diferente: stop, remember, forget, try.
