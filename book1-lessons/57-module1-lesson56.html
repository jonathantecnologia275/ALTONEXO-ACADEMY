Introduction · Introducción

Contractions combine two words into one shorter word using an apostrophe. Native speakers use them constantly in conversation — without them, spoken English sounds stiff and unnatural.

Las contracciones combinan dos palabras en una más corta usando un apóstrofe. Los hablantes nativos las usan constantemente en la conversación — sin ellas, el inglés hablado suena rígido y poco natural.

Did you know? / ¿Sabías qué?

"Won't" is the contraction of "will not" — it's one of the few contractions that doesn't simply remove letters and add an apostrophe in the same spot.

"Won't" es la contracción de "will not" — es una de las pocas contracciones que no simplemente quita letras y agrega un apóstrofe en el mismo lugar.

Pronunciation · Pronunciación
won't /woʊnt/
it's vs its

Don't confuse "it's" (it is / it has) with "its" (possessive, no apostrophe): "It's raining" vs. "The dog wagged its tail." / No confundas "it's" (it is / it has) con "its" (posesivo, sin apóstrofe): "It's raining" vs. "The dog wagged its tail."

"Their going to the party" (confusing there/their/they're)
"They're going to the party" (contraction of 'they are')
6
Grammar · Formal vs informal contexts

Use contractions in speech, texting, and informal writing ("I'm tired"). Avoid them in formal or academic writing ("I am tired") — full forms sound more professional in reports, essays, and official letters.

Usa contracciones al hablar, enviar mensajes de texto, y en escritura informal ("I'm tired"). Evítalas en escritura formal o académica ("I am tired") — las formas completas suenan más profesionales en reportes, ensayos, y cartas oficiales.
